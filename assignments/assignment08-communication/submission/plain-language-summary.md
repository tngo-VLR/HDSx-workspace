# Plain-Language Summary

In a prepared classroom dataset based on NHANES public-use records, mean BMI differed modestly across three income groups among adults ages 20-80. The low-income group had a mean BMI of 29.4, the middle-income group had a mean BMI of 29.3, and the high-income group had a mean BMI of 28.6. The corresponding group sizes were 15,293, 19,228, and 16,330 records. The Table 1 values show an observed pattern, but they do not explain why BMI differs or establish that income causes the differences.

This summary is for public-health practitioners and community partners who may discuss health-equity context. It is not a national estimate because the values are unweighted. It also is not a clinical finding for individual people. Before the grouped summary, missing BMI or income-group values were excluded, and no statistical significance test or adjustment for other factors was performed.

The appropriate next step is a careful conversation about the social and health opportunities represented in the data, followed by a transparent analysis that considers other variables and avoids small or unsupported claims. The finding should prompt questions and further analysis, not a claim that income alone determines BMI.
