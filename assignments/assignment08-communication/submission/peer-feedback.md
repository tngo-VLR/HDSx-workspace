# Peer Feedback

## Reviewer

**Reviewer name:** Josh

## Clarity

**Feedback received:** “The low-income and high-income comparison is clear, but the first sentence could be read as saying the groups differ for a known reason.”

**Main takeaway:** Adults age 20-80 show a small descriptive difference in mean BMI across income groups, without evidence that income caused the difference.

## Evidence

**Feedback received:** “Keep the exact N and BMI mean (SD) values on one slide so a reader can check the claim instead of relying on a rounded takeaway.”

**Evidence in the draft:** Assignment 6 Table 1, with N and BMI mean (SD) for each income group.

## Limitation

**Feedback received:** “The limitation slide should state why the public-health audience cannot use this as a population estimate.”

**Limitation in the draft:** The table is unweighted and complete-case descriptive data, with no significance test, adjustment, causal interpretation, or population-weighted estimate.

## KT Value

**Feedback received:** “The next step should invite discussion rather than name an intervention that this evidence does not support.”

**Audience need:** Public-health practitioners and community partners need a cautious starting point for health-equity discussion.

## Actionable Revision

**Before:** “Adults in the low-income group had a slightly higher mean BMI than adults in the high-income group.”

**After:** “In this prepared classroom dataset, adults in the low-income group had a slightly higher mean BMI than adults in the high-income group. This is an observed descriptive pattern, not evidence of a causal effect or a national estimate.”

I revised the takeaway wording and kept the evidence table visible to make the limited claim and supporting values easier to check.
